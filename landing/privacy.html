Application Privacy Policy

BatMonitor · Last updated: November 7, 2026 · Version 1.0

Summary. BatMonitor has no accounts and no servers of its own for your data. The batteries you save and the last reading of each are stored only on your phone. The app never sends your BMS readings, your battery names or your Bluetooth identifiers to its developer. The only network communications made by the app itself are: (1) on Android, a version check with no personal data in the request body, and (2) on Android, AidZap advertising, which may process technical advertising data (IP address, cookies, browser identifiers, etc.) under its own policy. The iOS app has no third-party advertising. If you do not want to share anything with the ad network, turn off your connection or uninstall the app: reading over Bluetooth does not need the internet.

1. Data controller

The controller of the data described in this policy is andyechc, the personal developer of BatMonitor, reachable through his personal site (andyechc.is-a.dev) and the project's public GitHub repository (github.com/andyechc/batmonitor), where you can open an issue or query. There is no company behind BatMonitor and no appointed data protection officer: it is a personal project with no user registration. BatMonitor is not affiliated with any BMS manufacturer.

2. Scope

This policy covers the BatMonitor apps for Android and iOS and the official download website (andyechc.is-a.dev/batmonitor). It does not cover battery manufacturers' firmware or services, nor the privacy policies of Uptodown, GitHub, AidZap, Apple or Google, which are governed by their own documents and are linked in section 8.

3. What data is processed and how

3.1 Data you save, stored on your phone

When you add a battery, the app stores locally, in the app's private storage:

  • The name you assign to the battery (by default, the name advertised by the BMS).
  • The Bluetooth identifier (UUID) of the BMS device, needed to reconnect to it. On Android the stored identifier is derived from the device's Bluetooth address; on iOS it is provided by CoreBluetooth.
  • The date you added the battery.
  • The last real reading received from that battery: state of charge (SOC %), voltage, current and reception date/time. This lets the app show the battery in the list even when it is not connected.

This data is kept in the system's private preferences (SharedPreferences on Android, UserDefaults on iOS). It is never sent to any developer server.

3.2 BMS data read live

When you connect a battery, the app reads over Bluetooth LE, in real time and only while connected: total voltage, current, remaining and nominal capacity, cycles, the date recorded by the BMS, each cell's voltage, NTC sensor temperatures, active protections, charge/discharge switch (FET) states, cell balancing state, numbers of cells and sensors, and the BMS firmware version. This data is only shown on your screen: apart from the last reading in 3.1, it is not stored permanently and is not transmitted to third parties.

Control actions (changing the name stored in the BMS, enabling automatic balancing, clearing protections and opening/closing the charge and discharge switches) are sent over Bluetooth only to the BMS you selected. None of these actions leaves the local link with your battery.

3.3 Bluetooth scan data

While you search for batteries, the app keeps in memory the devices it finds: advertised name, UUID and signal strength (RSSI). These results are not saved: only the data of the battery you choose to add is kept, under the terms of 3.1.

3.4 Network data from the update check (Android only)

When you open the app, BatMonitor for Android makes a single GET request to https://andyechc.is-a.dev/batmonitor/api/version.json to see whether a new version is available. The request carries no personal data in its body: your name, battery, device identifier and installed version are not sent. As with any HTTPS request, the server/CDN hosting the page (GitHub Pages) may log what is usual in a web log: IP address, date and time, and the system user agent. This is used only to serve the file and check the version.

The iOS app does not perform this check or any other network request: its code contains no network calls.

3.5 Data BatMonitor does NOT collect

The app itself does not collect or transmit: accounts, names, email addresses, phone numbers, contacts, photos, files, microphone, camera, GPS location, system advertising identifiers (Android Advertising ID / IDFA), health data, financial data, or browsing data across apps. It contains no analytics, audience measurement, or crash reporting of its own.

5. Retention, backups and deletion

Your local data is kept until you delete it: removing a battery from the app deletes its name, identifier and last reading; uninstalling the app deletes all local data. Please note:

  • On Android the app allows system backup (allowBackup), so your saved batteries may be included in your device backup if you have it enabled. That backup is governed by Google's terms.
  • On iOS, UserDefaults data may be included in your iCloud backup if you have it enabled, under Apple's terms.
  • Backups may retain data deleted on the phone until the system itself overwrites or removes it; BatMonitor has no control over that cycle.

6. Purposes and legal basis

  • Providing the service (reading and controlling your BMS over Bluetooth): performance of the user's own request when installing and using the app.
  • Maintenance and security (checking for a new version): legitimate interest in offering updates and fixing bugs.
  • Advertising (AidZap ads on Android and on the website): shown as the counterpart of a free app. Advertising does not use your battery or BMS reading data. You can stop seeing it by turning off your internet connection or uninstalling the app.

7. Third-party advertising: AidZap

The Android app shows AidZap ads (aidzap.com) in a fixed WebView (banner and native ads), and the download website shows its banner and native ads. When they load, the WebView or browser contacts AidZap's servers, so AidZap and its advertising partners may process, for their own purposes and under their own privacy policy, technical data such as your IP address, user agent, cookies or WebView storage, device or browser identifiers, approximate location derived from the IP, and ad interactions. BatMonitor never sends AidZap your battery names, Bluetooth identifiers, BMS readings, or any account data (there is no account). The developer does not control which specific data AidZap processes or for how long: see AidZap's privacy policy at aidzap.com. The iOS app shows no third-party advertising.

9. Download website

The page andyechc.is-a.dev/batmonitor is a static page with no accounts and no forms. When you visit it, the hosting provider (GitHub Pages) may log the usual technical data of a web visit, and the page's AidZap ads are governed by section 7. 10. Your rights

You can exercise your rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection by writing through the contact channels in section 1. In practice, most are exercised directly on your phone: removing a battery deletes its saved data, and uninstalling the app deletes all local data. For any data AidZap or GitHub may have logged in their own systems, please contact those third parties under their policies. You may also lodge a complaint with the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) or the supervisory authority corresponding to your place of residence.

11. Security

Local data is stored in the app's private storage, protected by the operating system. The version check and ads run over HTTPS. BatMonitor does not transmit your BMS readings to any server, so there is no central database that could be breached. The Bluetooth link is local between your phone and your BMS. Note that your device backup (section 5) depends on the configuration and security of your Apple or Google account.

12. Children

BatMonitor is not directed at children and does not deliberately collect data from any user by age. Its only audience is people who own BMS batteries and a compatible phone.

Minor changes will also be published here. We recommend reviewing this page periodically.
